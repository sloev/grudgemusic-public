The Account 1909

Mardiros Tashjian, “Dalvorig” (Columbia, 1909)

Background: A pre-1909 English-language text witness is Alice Stone Blackwell's Armenian Poems (Boston: Roberts Brothers, 1896), which includes “The Son of Dalvorig,” attributed to Mihran Damadian, pp. 135–138. The poem's speaker identifies himself as a free son of Dalvorig, refuses to bow before the Turk, calls Armenian brothers to join him for liberty, rejects foreign domination, and describes armed struggle. In its closing movement, the speaker says that for every blow dealt him, he returns a thousand.

Proposed role: Anti-subjugation / armed defiance / collective liberation

Why it fits / decision: Strong fit for anti-subjugation, collective defiance and armed resistance. The text is not a neutral folk outlaw song: it belongs to a specific Armenian political and historical repertoire. The archive should contextualize its revolutionary language, identify the historical actors as carefully as possible, and avoid turning a song-family text into a claim about the exact 1909 performance before transcription.
