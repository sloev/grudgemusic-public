Out of Turn Rio de Janeiro, Brazil · 1917

A samba that mocked the police chief

Samba grew up in Rio's Black neighbourhoods, around houses like Tia Ciata's, under constant police pressure. In January 1917 Casa Edison released "Pelo Telefone", credited to Donga and sung by Baiano on Odeon 121322. It was the hit of that year's carnival.

The popular version opens with the police chief phoning to say the fun can go ahead, a dig at police corruption hidden in a party song. A music the police harassed became Brazil's first samba hit, and its first line was a joke at the police's expense.
