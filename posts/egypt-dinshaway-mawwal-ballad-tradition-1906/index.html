Egypt: Dinshaway mawwal / ballad tradition (1906)

Background: Ziad Fahmy's study Ordinary Egyptians: Creating the Modern Nation through Popular Culture describes the near-immediate commemoration of the 1906 Dinshaway incident in rural colloquial Egyptian mawāwīl (singular mawwāl, ballad-like sung narratives). The book reports that these songs circulated widely in the countryside and that many were probably not composed by a single identifiable writer. One mawwāl is attributed to Sheikh Mustafa ʿAjāj.

Why it fits / decision: The reported ballad appears to imagine retaliatory humiliation or punishment of the colonial authority as a response to an injury understood as dishonouring. It may be closer to retaliatory justice or collective vengeance than to a private, calculated grudge. The exact wording and narrative sequence must be checked against Kamal's Arabic transcript before fixing the taxonomy.
